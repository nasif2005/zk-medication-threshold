SECTION 6.3 PRIVACY QUANTIFICATION EXPERIMENT
============================================================

1. PURPOSE
----------

This experiment quantifies the privacy provided by three medication-threshold
enforcement architectures:

1. Centralized plaintext-history enforcement.
2. Trusted encrypted-database enforcement.
3. Proposed zk-SNARK hidden-state enforcement.

All three architectures are evaluated using exactly the same generated patient
histories. Consequently, differences in the privacy measurements arise from the
information exposed by each architecture, rather than differences in their input
populations.

The experiment is separate from the Section 6.2.3 performance benchmark. It does
not modify the existing performance code or previously reported latency results.


2. MEDICATION POLICY AND FIXED PARAMETERS
-----------------------------------------

The experiment uses the following fixed policy and request parameters, matching
the primary configuration evaluated in Section 6.2.3:

- Bounded-history capacity, k: 10 entries
- Medication identifier: 42
- Current requested quantity, q_current: 10 quantity units
- Current request time, T_current: 100 time units
- Medication threshold, m_q: 60 quantity units
- Rolling-window length, W: 30 time units

An earlier dispensing event is active when its timestamp satisfies:

    T_current - W < t_i <= T_current

Under the selected parameters, this becomes:

    70 < t_i <= 100

The left boundary is strict. An event at time 70 is expired, whereas an event at
time 71 is active.

The request is authorized when the prior active-window quantity plus the current
requested quantity does not exceed the threshold:

    X + q_current <= m_q

Because q_current = 10 and m_q = 60, the rule becomes:

    X + 10 <= 60

Therefore, requests with X <= 50 are authorized and requests with X > 50 are
rejected. Equality with the medication threshold is permitted.


3. COMMON SENSITIVE VARIABLE
----------------------------

The protected variable is the patient's total prior quantity within the active
rolling window:

    X = S_active
      = sum_i q_i * 1[T_current - W < t_i <= T_current]

Here, q_i and t_i are the quantity and timestamp of prior dispensing event i,
and 1[condition] is one when the condition is true and zero otherwise.

The experiment protects and predicts this scalar quantity rather than attempting
to infer the complete ordered medication history. This makes the privacy metric
directly relevant to the threshold decision and gives ALI, mutual information,
and adversary success probability a common target.


4. ADVERSARY AND TRUST MODEL
----------------------------

The experiment assumes an honest-but-curious internal observer. The observer
performs the required enforcement operation correctly but uses everything visible
inside its architectural trust boundary to infer X.

The observer may know the public policy and request values and the authorization
outcome. The observer does not compromise the patient-side application and does
not break the encryption, commitment, hash, or zk-SNARK mechanisms.

For the trusted encrypted-database architecture, the evaluated observer is inside
the trusted evaluation/decryption boundary. Encryption at rest protects against
an outside storage observer, but the trusted evaluator must decrypt the history
to enforce the policy. Thus, that evaluator can calculate X exactly.

For the proposed architecture, the observer is the Medication Enforcement
Authority (MEA). The MEA receives the public proof statement and authorization
outcome but does not receive the prior quantities or timestamps.


5. OBSERVABLE VARIABLE FOR EACH ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------

Let H represent the prior medication history, Z the fixed public policy/request
context, and B the binary authorization outcome:

    Z = (q_current, T_current, m_q, W, medication_id)

    B = 1[X + q_current <= m_q]

The evaluated observations are:

Centralized plaintext enforcement:

    Y_plain = (H, Z, B)

The enforcement service directly receives the history and can compute X exactly.

Trusted encrypted-database enforcement:

    Y_encrypted = (H, Z, B)

The records may be encrypted while stored, but the trusted evaluator observes the
decrypted history during evaluation and can compute X exactly.

Proposed zk-SNARK hidden-state enforcement:

    Y_zk = (Z, B)

The MEA observes the fixed public request and policy values and the authorization
outcome, but not H or X.

Cryptographic proofs, commitments, Merkle roots, and nullifiers are not inserted
as raw categorical values into the empirical mutual-information estimator. Under
the stated cryptographic assumptions, these values are opaque with respect to the
private history. Because they are normally unique, treating their encodings as
ordinary categories would make them act as sample identifiers and would produce
a misleading empirical leakage estimate. Proof-specific confidentiality is instead
supported by the zero-knowledge assumption.


6. SHARED WORKLOAD GENERATION
-----------------------------

The default experiment generates 10,000 histories using pseudorandom seed
20260813. The generator records the seed and all fixed parameters in
workload/metadata.json.

For every history:

1. The number of active entries is sampled uniformly from 0 through 5.
2. The number of expired entries is sampled from 0 through the smaller of 3 and
   the remaining available capacity, while reserving at least one replaceable
   empty slot.
3. Each occupied entry receives an integer quantity sampled uniformly from 1
   through 20.
4. Active timestamps are sampled from 71 through 100.
5. Expired timestamps are sampled from 1 through 70.
6. Occupied entries are shuffled, and unused positions are encoded as (0, 0).
7. X is calculated from the generated quantities and timestamps.
8. B is calculated using X + 10 <= 60.

The generated rows are saved once in workload/shared_histories.csv. All three
architectural observations are derived from these same rows. No architecture
receives a separately sampled or transformed input population.

This workload is synthetic and is designed to exercise the privacy metrics. Its
sampling distribution is not claimed to represent a clinical dispensing
population.


7. PRIVACY METRICS
------------------

7.1 Amount of Leaked Information (ALI)

ALI uses one scalar-level disclosure unit. It records whether the exact selected
sensitive value X is directly available to the observer:

    ALI = 1, if exact X is directly disclosed or exactly computable from Y
    ALI = 0, otherwise

Under this definition, plaintext and trusted encrypted-database enforcement have
ALI = 1. The zk-SNARK architecture has ALI = 0 because the MEA does not receive
the history or exact active-window quantity.

This is a scalar-level ALI definition, not a count of every disclosed history
record or field.

7.2 Mutual Information (MI)

Empirical mutual information measures the statistical dependence between X and Y:

    I(X;Y) = sum_x sum_y P(x,y) log2(P(x,y)/(P(x)P(y)))

The probabilities are empirical frequencies over the complete shared workload.
MI is reported in bits. Lower MI indicates that the observation reveals less
information about X.

Because the plaintext and trusted evaluators observe the history, X is a
deterministic function of their observations. Their MI therefore equals the
empirical entropy H(X).

For the zk-SNARK architecture, all elements of Z are fixed in the primary
experiment, so variation in Y_zk is caused only by B. Its measured leakage is
therefore I(X;B), which cannot exceed one bit for a binary outcome.

7.3 Expected Adversary Success Probability (ASP)

ASP is the expected probability that an optimal empirical adversary correctly
guesses the exact value of X after seeing Y:

    ASP = sum_y P(y) max_x P(X=x | Y=y)

The calculation aggregates over all observations in the workload, rather than a
single successful proof or request.

The prior-only guessing probability is also reported:

    ASP_prior = max_x P(X=x)

The inference advantage supplied by an architecture is:

    ASP_advantage = ASP - ASP_prior

Plaintext and trusted encrypted-database enforcement produce ASP = 1 because X is
exactly computable from the observed history. For the zk-SNARK architecture, ASP
is calculated by grouping histories according to the observed authorization
outcome and choosing the most frequent X within each outcome group.


8. EXECUTION PROCEDURE
----------------------

Run the complete experiment from the 6.3_privacy_quantification directory with:

    python scripts/run_experiment.py

The default command performs two steps:

1. generate_workload.py creates the seeded common workload.
2. analyze_privacy.py validates the workload, constructs each observer view,
   computes the metrics, and joins the privacy results with available Section
   6.2.3 performance measurements.

The sample count and seed can be changed explicitly, for example:

    python scripts/run_experiment.py --samples 20000 --seed 20260813


9. VALIDATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY CHECKS
----------------------------------------

Before calculating metrics, the analysis verifies that:

- every row uses the same public request and policy parameters;
- each history contains exactly k = 10 quantities and timestamps;
- the stored X equals the value recomputed from the history;
- the stored authorization outcome equals the threshold rule;
- the workload contains at least two distinct X values; and
- both authorized and rejected observations are present.

The default workload was regenerated with the recorded seed and produced an
identical SHA-256 file hash, confirming deterministic reproduction in the current
environment.


10. DEFAULT EXPERIMENT RESULTS
------------------------------

The default seed and 10,000-sample configuration produced:

- Distinct X values: 91
- Minimum X: 0
- Maximum X: 95
- Authorized observations: 8,628
- Rejected observations: 1,372
- Empirical entropy H(X): 5.718815 bits
- Prior-only ASP: 0.1679

Architecture                         ALI     MI (bits)    Expected ASP
-----------------------------------  ------  -----------  ------------
Centralized plaintext                1       5.718815     1.0000
Trusted encrypted database           1       5.718815     1.0000
Proposed zk-SNARK hidden state        0       0.576859     0.1785

The zk-SNARK ASP advantage over the prior-only guess is:

    0.1785 - 0.1679 = 0.0106

These results show that the plaintext and trusted evaluators completely learn X
within the evaluated trust boundary. The MEA does not directly receive X. Its
remaining measured information comes from the binary authorization outcome.


11. PRIVACY-PERFORMANCE COMPARISON
----------------------------------

The analysis joins its privacy results with the mean processing times already
reported in Section 6.2.3:

- Centralized plaintext mean latency: 0.000002449548 seconds
  (approximately 2.450 microseconds)
- Proposed zk-SNARK mean end-to-end cryptographic latency: 4.339075 seconds
- Trusted encrypted-database latency: not measured

The trusted encrypted-database latency is explicitly recorded as not_measured.
The experiment does not invent or interpolate a value that was not measured by
the existing evaluation code.

The comparison therefore demonstrates the measured trade-off between the very
low latency and complete disclosure of plaintext enforcement and the substantially
higher cryptographic latency but lower observer-side leakage of the proposed
framework. A complete three-way performance comparison requires a separately
implemented and measured trusted encrypted-database baseline.


12. OUTPUT FILES
----------------

- workload/shared_histories.csv
  The common generated histories and derived ground-truth X and B values.

- workload/metadata.json
  The seed, sample count, fixed parameters, and sensitive-variable definition.

- results/architecture_observations.csv
  The observation Y constructed for each architecture and every shared history.

- results/privacy_metrics.csv
  ALI, empirical MI, expected ASP, prior ASP, ASP advantage, H(X), and sample
  count for each architecture.

- results/privacy_performance_tradeoff.csv
  The privacy metrics combined with the available Section 6.2.3 mean latencies.

- results/summary.json
  A machine-readable summary of the workload and results.


13. INTERPRETATION LIMITATIONS
------------------------------

1. The generated histories are synthetic. Metric values depend on the selected
   history distribution and should not be presented as population-level clinical
   estimates.

2. MI and ASP quantify leakage about the selected scalar X, not the complete
   medication history, patient identity, or other sensitive attributes.

3. The zk-SNARK result does not imply zero total leakage. The binary authorization
   outcome is intentionally public and reveals whether X is at most 50 under the
   fixed request and policy.

4. Repeated requests with varying quantities, times, or thresholds could reveal
   more information than this single-request, fixed-context experiment. Such an
   adaptive or longitudinal observer is outside the current experiment.

5. Side channels, including timing, network metadata, compromised patient devices,
   and failures of cryptographic assumptions, are outside the evaluated model.

6. The encrypted-database conclusion is observer-relative. It applies to a trusted
   evaluator inside the decryption boundary. An external storage-only adversary
   would have a different observable view and different privacy measurements.

7. The reported MI and ASP values are empirical plug-in estimates. Additional
   uncertainty analysis or bootstrap confidence intervals may be added if required
   for the final manuscript.
