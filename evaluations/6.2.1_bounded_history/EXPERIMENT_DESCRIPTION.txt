SECTION 6.2.1 - EFFECT OF BOUNDED-HISTORY CAPACITY
=================================================

1. PURPOSE
----------

This experiment measures how the bounded private-history capacity k affects the
size and online execution cost of the proposed Groth16 medication-threshold
state-transition circuit. It isolates k by holding the Merkle-tree depth, policy,
request, and logical workload fixed while compiling separate circuits for
k = 10, 15, and 20.


2. INDEPENDENT AND DEPENDENT VARIABLES
--------------------------------------

Independent variable:

    k in {10, 15, 20}

Dependent variables:

- compiled R1CS constraint count;
- number of private and public inputs;
- witness-generation latency;
- Groth16 proof-generation latency;
- Groth16 proof-verification latency; and
- total prover latency, defined as witness plus proof generation.

Merkle-tree depth is fixed at d = 2.


3. FIXED MEDICATION WORKLOAD
----------------------------

Every configuration uses the same policy and request values:

- q_current = 10 quantity units
- T_current = 100 time units
- m_q = 60 quantity units
- W = 30 time units
- medication identifier = 42

An event is active when 70 < t_i <= 100. Each prior history begins with:

    quantities: [20, 15, 5]
    timestamps: [90, 80, 60]

The first two events are active, giving a prior active sum of 35. The third event
is expired. All remaining positions up to capacity k are canonical empty entries
(0, 0). The current request is valid because 35 + 10 <= 60.

The expired entry at index 2 is replaced with (10, 100) in the successor state.
Thus, every configuration exercises active/expired classification, rolling-window
aggregation, overwrite-slot eligibility, predecessor commitment reconstruction,
successor commitment construction, nullifier derivation, and Merkle membership.

Only the number of additional empty history slots changes with k.


4. CIRCUIT AND CRYPTOGRAPHIC OPERATIONS
---------------------------------------

The Circom relation checks:

1. reconstruction of the predecessor state commitment;
2. binding between the patient-medication tag and medication identifier;
3. temporal classification of all k history entries;
4. aggregation of active quantities;
5. compliance of the current request with the threshold;
6. eligibility of the selected empty or expired overwrite slot;
7. correctness of the successor history and commitment;
8. derivation of the predecessor nullifier; and
9. membership of the predecessor commitment in the public Merkle root.

Groth16 is instantiated over the BN128 curve. Each k value has a separate compiled
circuit, WebAssembly witness generator, proving key, and verification key.


5. CIRCUIT SIZE
---------------

Configuration   Constraints   Private inputs   Public inputs
--------------  ------------  ---------------  -------------
k = 10          26,716        50               8
k = 15          37,686        70               8
k = 20          48,656        90               8

Increasing k from 10 to 20 adds 21,940 constraints, an increase of approximately
82.1 percent. Public-input count remains fixed because additional history entries
are private witness values.


6. MEASUREMENT PROCEDURE
------------------------

For each k configuration, the benchmark performs:

1. five unrecorded warm-up executions;
2. thirty recorded executions;
3. witness generation in a fresh Node.js command;
4. Groth16 proof generation in a fresh SnarkJS command; and
5. Groth16 proof verification in a fresh SnarkJS command.

Elapsed wall-clock time is measured using Python's high-resolution performance
counter. All proof and public-input artifacts are preserved by configuration and
run number. Circuit compilation, Powers-of-Tau processing, and proving-key
generation are treated as one-time preprocessing and excluded from online latency.

The saved environment reports Ubuntu/Linux on x86-64, Python 3.10.12, 30 measured
runs, and 5 warm-ups. The manuscript additionally identifies the host as a Google
Cloud e2-standard-4 virtual machine with four vCPUs and 16 GB RAM, using Circom
2.2.3, Node.js 20.20.2, and SnarkJS 0.7.6.


7. RESULTS
----------

Mean latencies over 30 measured runs:

k       Witness (s)   Prove (s)   Verify (s)   Total prover (s)
------  ------------  ----------  -----------  ----------------
10      0.1280        3.5441      0.6261       3.6721
15      0.4165        5.3074      0.6380       5.7238
20      0.4070        5.8595      0.6306       6.2665

From k = 10 to k = 20, mean proof-generation time rises by approximately 65.3
percent and mean total prover time rises by approximately 70.7 percent. Verification
remains close to 0.63 seconds because Groth16 uses a constant-size proof and the
public-input structure does not change.

The k = 15 witness and proof measurements show greater variation than the other
configurations. Therefore, the supplied summary includes standard deviation,
median, minimum, and maximum in addition to the arithmetic mean.


8. REPRODUCTION
---------------

The scripts are intended to be run after installing the pinned Node dependencies.
First build all circuit variants with scripts/build_variants.sh, then run:

    python3 scripts/benchmark_variants.py --runs 30 --warmups 5

The benchmark fails if a required input, WebAssembly circuit, witness generator,
proving key, or verification key is missing.


9. OUTPUTS
----------

- inputs/k10_valid.json, k15_valid.json, k20_valid.json:
  deterministic valid circuit inputs.
- build/k*/r1cs_info.txt:
  circuit-size and input-count information.
- results/raw_measurements.csv:
  every recorded timing observation.
- results/summary.csv:
  mean, sample standard deviation, median, minimum, and maximum.
- results/environment.json:
  execution environment and run counts.
- results/artifacts/k*/run_*/:
  witnesses, proofs, and public inputs for individual runs.


10. INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------

The results characterize component-level cryptographic processing for one valid,
deterministic synthetic workload. They isolate capacity-related circuit cost but
do not measure differences among clinical history distributions. The observed
witness timings are not strictly monotonic, although circuit constraints and mean
proof-generation cost increase with k.

The experiment excludes compilation, trusted setup, network communication,
persistent production storage, concurrency, user-interface processing, service
orchestration, and institutional integration. It should not be interpreted as an
end-to-end healthcare deployment benchmark.
